# SmartMind (University of Moratuwa): website content brief

Oct 14, 2026 · @Sandaruwan

SmartMind is a GeoAI network of Town & Country Planning alumni and students at the University of Moratuwa, run through the Urban Simulation Lab. I found no dedicated SmartMind website, only a Facebook page and a section on the lab's university page, so this brief collects everything public that a site would need.

## What SmartMind is

SmartMind is a virtual network of Town & Country Planning alumni and students, united by applying GeoAI to urban simulation and planning. The University of Moratuwa lists it on the [Urban Simulation Lab page](https://uom.lk/tcp/urban-simulation-lab) of the Department of Town & Country Planning.

| Item | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Name | SmartMind (a LinkedIn post by a team member spells the group name "Smarmind Research Group", which looks like a typo) |
| Home | Urban Simulation Lab, Department of Town & Country Planning, Faculty of Architecture, University of Moratuwa, Sri Lanka |
| Lab tagline | "Envisaging Cities: Makes Simulation Simplified" |
| Core idea | Cities are now systems of systems, so conventional simulation hits its limits; SmartMind aims to simplify and reimagine how future cities are simulated |
| Call to action on the lab page | Join SmartMind on its journey |
| Social | [Facebook link on the lab page](https://www.facebook.com/share/12KWJGHiFm3/?mibextid=wwXIfr) |
| University contact | Bandaranayake Mawatha, Moratuwa 10400 · +94 11 2640051 / +94 11 2650301 · info@uom.lk |

SmartMind is not related to other organisations of the same name: smartmind.net (a Spanish e-learning company), smartmind.ro (a Romanian HR consultancy) and the SMARTMIND neuroscience programme at UTEP. Worth knowing before choosing a domain and writing SEO copy.

## Chatbot journey

SmartMind's most visible work is a line of AI chatbots, moving from urban-planning Q&A (2023) to disaster-awareness bots for landslides and floods (2023 to 2026). Newest first.

| When | Milestone | Detail |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 22–24 Sep 2026 | Street Cloud at the 4th IOER Conference, Dresden | Citizens share ideas alongside street imagery; generative and vision AI produce redesign scenarios; planners and people iterate together. Supported by the Alexander von Humboldt Foundation and DAAD, hosted by TU Dresden's Chair of Mobility System Planning. Source: team LinkedIn post. |
| 20–22 May 2026 | Participatory flood and landslide chatbot, 1st International Online Conference on Urban Sciences | WhatsApp-based, multilingual, multi-agent design (language, live-weather API and scheduler agents). Gives residents preparedness guidance and lets planners interpret risk-sensitive zoning. Linked to SDG 11 and 13, and indirectly 9 and 17. Published 15 May 2026 on [Sciforum](https://sciforum.net/paper/31118). |
| May 2025 | Best Scientific Paper, The Colombo Conference 2025 (ICSD-2025) | Paper on a collaborative approach to disaster management and community awareness, featuring the AI chatbot built for NBRO. Source: a team member's LinkedIn profile. |
| 29 Dec 2023 | Meeting with NBRO and the University of Manchester, UK | Agreed a research project on an AI chatbot for landslide risk: early warnings, evacuation protocols, resilient-construction details and risk information. Supported by a Manchester grant. See the [NBRO news item](https://nbro.gov.lk/index.php?Itemid=190&catid=8&id=564%3Anational-building-research-organisation-nbro-the-smartmind-team-from-university-of-moratuwa-and-manchester-university-uk-are-collaborating-in-order-to-develop-a-chatbot&lang=en&option=com_content&view=article) and the [chatbot article](https://thechatbot.net/urban-planning-chatbot-sri-lanka/). |
| 2023 (after EXMO) | Chatbot 2.0 | Built on the Hugging Chat language model with web search, tuned response filters, a user-friendly GUI and a "Pro Planning Mode". A custom version was made for Port City Colombo. |
| 2023 | First chatbot launched at EXMO 2023, University of Moratuwa | Trained on real district development plans; visitor feedback fed a published paper. |

The stated philosophy is institution-specific chatbots that handle large workloads quickly and reduce dependence on individual officials. The team invites collaboration to build tailored chatbots for individuals and institutions, which suggests a "Collaborate with us" page.

## Research areas

The Urban Simulation Lab groups its ongoing research into four themes, with four projects marked as funded by the university's SRC.

| Theme | Projects listed |
| --- | --- |
| Land use and population | Urban population modelling (SRC-funded); level of vegetation land fragmentation in the Western Province (SRC-funded) |
| Flood | Framework linking land use, accessibility, density and surface runoff as a decision tool for planners (SRC-funded); urban flood modelling app testing how building regulations cope with uncertain rainfall (SRC-funded) |
| Traffic and transport | Road crash magnitude versus road network configuration, Kegalle; built environment versus travellers' mode choice, Colombo |
| AI and machine learning | A deep-learning framework for road inventory systems; deep learning for road inventories and 3D urban simulation |

The department's [ongoing research page](https://uom.lk/tcp/research/ongoing-research) adds seven wider topics: population distribution simulation, building density and zoning versus runoff, site-level land use and transport interaction, an AI-based landslide simulation model for Sri Lanka, land fragmentation and urban sprawl, citizen-science city mapping, and a web-based decision system for Indo-Lankan cities.

One named project, **4ONSE**, is a low-cost weather station built on open hardware (Arduino), open software (istSOS), open data (CKAN) and OGC standards. It measures eight environmental parameters, and 27 stations were deployed in the Deduru Oya basin. It is a joint project with the University of Applied Sciences and Arts of Southern Switzerland (SUPSI); the page lists it under the department, so confirm with the team whether SmartMind should claim it.

## Gaps and open questions

Public sources give enough text for the site but not its brand, contacts or visuals. Settle these before design starts.

- **Purpose and audience:** Is the site for recruiting alumni and students, showcasing research, or finding chatbot clients? This decides the home page.
- **Brand:** Confirm the spelling "SmartMind", and supply a logo and colours. None were found.
- **Contact:** No SmartMind email, domain or LinkedIn page turned up. The lab page links only a Facebook share link.
- **Team:** Roles, photos and consent to be listed are needed for all eight core members.
- **Chatbots:** Is any bot publicly live (a WhatsApp number or link)? Screenshots on the 2024 article belong to its publisher and the team, so ask before reuse.
- **Languages:** The 2026 chatbot is multilingual. Decide whether the site is English only or also Sinhala and Tamil.
- **Claims to confirm:** Whether SmartMind may claim the 4ONSE station, the Port City bot and the NBRO and Manchester partnership, and whether those organisations allow their names and logos.
- **Not read:** The Facebook page could not be read by automated tools, and LinkedIn profiles were seen only as search snippets, so anything only there (events, member lists) is missing.
